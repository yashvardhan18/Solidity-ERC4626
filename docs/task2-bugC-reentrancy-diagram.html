The reentrancy exploit, call by call

BrokenVault.redeem() pays a withdrawer their tokens before it burns their shares. The token used here notifies its recipient the instant it receives money — and if the recipient is a contract, that notification can contain new instructions. The diagram below traces exactly what those instructions do: a second call, nested inside the first one, that buys new shares while the vault's books still describe a version of reality that no longer exists.

Net effect: 100 tokens go in, ≈109.9 come back out. The extra ≈9.9 was never new money — it's a slice of Bob's 900-token claim, the only other depositor in the vault, taken without his knowledge because the vault let its own bookkeeping fall out of sync with reality for the length of one token transfer.

The fix (FixedVault.sol) closes this two ways: _burn now runs before the token transfer, so the books are already correct by the time any hook can fire — and every entry point also carries a nonReentrant guard, so even if a future change ever got the ordering wrong again, the nested deposit() call in R5 would simply revert instead of succeeding.
